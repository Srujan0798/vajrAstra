Runs the **South / AksharDrishti** Indic OCR campaign — a 10-day hackathon aimed at beating Sarvam (87.39 benchmark), Gnani, and an internal rival plan ("Vinay's plan") that is tracking toward million-dollar funding. He has said he is staking his entire life on it.

**How he works:**
- **Solo, with agents as the workforce.** His mentor's line — "there is no need of people, just AI itself is enough, but we need the correct methods and correct architecture" — is the operating thesis. Multi-agent orchestration *is* his competitive edge, not a convenience.
- **Model tiering is deliberate.** He uses Opus for high-level planning, protocol design and architecture, then switches to Sonnet to execute with subagents. When he says "you are the boss," he means: decide, don't ask.
- **Writes directives as formal documents.** Long, numbered, box-drawn law files with concern registers (CO-001…CO-096), rule numbering, deliverable IDs. Expects the same register/evidence discipline back.
- **Communicates fast and unpolished** — short, typo-heavy, high-context messages. Read for intent, not spelling; the intent is usually precise even when the typing isn't.
- **Distrusts unverified agent reports.** He has been burned by agents claiming completion without disk evidence, which is why every protocol he writes demands cross-checking.

**What he wants from me:**
- Decisions with recommendations, not menus of questions.
- Evidence from disk over restatement of what a doc claims.
- Being countered when he's wrong — he wrote the standing order for it himself.
- Persistence to files, because he knows session memory dies mid-campaign.

**How to apply:** Lead with what I verified, then what I'd do, then the one genuine fork if there is one. Don't ask him to re-explain context that is recoverable from the repo or from [[campaign-state]].
