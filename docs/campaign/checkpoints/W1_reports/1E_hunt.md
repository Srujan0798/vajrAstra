## 1. FINDS

### F1 — The Santali cell is measuring Bengali, not Santali. `sat` is structurally saturated. **PRIMARY**
Ol Chiki is only **54.3%** of the characters in the `sat` GT; the rest is a **Bengali parallel rendering of the same question on the same page**. Verified on `sat_05`: `ᱪᱤᱥᱤ ᱠᱚᱛᱮ ᱚᱞ ᱟᱠᱟᱱᱟ?` + `চিকি কতে অল আকানা?`.
- A recogniser **perfect on Ol Chiki and silent on the Bengali scores CER = 1 − 0.543 = 0.457**; our best local engine on `sat` scores **0.459**. **The monolingual floor and the observed score are the same number.** The cell cannot move on better Ol Chiki modelling.
- Past the floor lies **script-block coverage, not script quality**: the only board row with `sat` CER < 0.1 is `sarvam_vision sat_05` at **0.026** (112 predicted vs 113 GT chars) — it cleared the floor by **transcribing both scripts**. n=3. DERIVED.
- **Why overlooked:** every Indic OCR benchmark scores a *page*; bilingual-raster minority-script pages are rare enough that nobody built a per-script-block metric. `level2/probe22` has them by accident.
- **We could build (1 day, no training, no download):** per-script-block CER — split GT and prediction into maximal single-Unicode-block runs, report `CER_olchiki` and `CER_bengali` separately beside page CER. This turns a **barred/dead** cell into the **highest-signal cell we own**: the only place on our board where we can prove an engine reads Ol Chiki at all.
- **Risk:** if the Bengali half is a *translation*, per-block alignment is ill-defined — report block **detection** instead. Checked on 3 items: it is a transliteration. Cost 1 day. Novelty: **PRIMARY**.

### F2 — Marathi ground truth is contaminated with Ol Chiki mojibake. ~+0.10 CER added to every engine's `mr` score. **PRIMARY**
18 of 79 scored `mr` items have **Ol Chiki injected into Devanagari words**: `शब्द` → `ᱫक`, `शाला` → `᭡ली`, `कायाही` → `कायᭅवाही`, `सुधारा` → `कृतᱭऐवजी` (645 Ol Chiki chars in the `mr` GT). A legacy font-decoding artefact, same class as the 3,486 `gu` mojibake rejections on record, that survived into `gt`.
- Effect: best engine CER **0.105 (n=61 clean) → 0.537–0.541 (n=18 contaminated)**; contribution to the 79-item mean ≈ (18/79) × (0.54 − 0.11) = **+0.098**.
- **We could build (0.5 day):** a GT script-purity gate — flag items whose script histogram contains a script that is not the language's expected script, a permitted neighbour, or punctuation; re-score `mr` both ways. Marathi was a target of the 2026-09-29 manifest additions (mr +21) — **more items from the same pipeline will add more of this artefact, not less.**
- Risk: `pa` 88/90 items contain Devanagari-block chars, but the context is only `।` U+0964 DANDA, which Gurmukhi legitimately uses — **the gate needs a punctuation whitelist or it will shred Punjabi.** Checked, not assumed. Cost 0.5 day. Novelty: **PRIMARY**.

### F3 — The fusion ceiling is real and measured; naive voting is the wrong mechanism. The edge is a **verifier behind the vote**. **DERIVED**
- Seeded 300-item probe22 sample (`random.seed(20260926)`), repo normalizer, 10 local engines: **best single surya 0.5229; per-item ORACLE over 10 engines 0.4277** (−0.095, −18% relative). The complementary information exists.
- **ROVER-lite (medoid anchor + per-character vote, difflib opcode alignment) = 0.5519 — WORSE than the best single engine**, and worse in 18/19 languages (`sat` +0.374, `hi` +0.290, `bn` +0.288). The reason is diagnosable: our engines disagree by emitting *different scripts* or *nothing*, so a character vote propagates the medoid's error.
- The mechanism that works is **LV-ROVER-MLT** (`https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00250` v5, 27 Aug 2026, **1st in the DocEng 2026 Maltese competition, held-out CER 0.0074** vs 0.0161 runner-up): anchor-preserving arbitration plus two **eligibility gates** — a lexicon gate and a diacritic-restoration gate. Its leave-one-out ablation (Table 3) makes the restoration gate the largest single recognition contributor (**+0.00386, paired bootstrap 95% CI 0.00266–0.00517, permutation p < 0.0001**); its own oracle is per-paragraph best-stream **0.01118 vs 0.01432** best fixed stream.
- **The Indic analogue of the lexicon gate is an akshara/orthography-validity verifier** — ~300 lines of rules over grapheme clusters (terminal virama, orphan ZWJ/ZWNJ, nukta on a letter with no nukta form, Malayalam chillu+virama, matra without a base). No training. It turns a character vote into a *gated* vote, and is the only mechanism I found plausibly worth the 0.095 gap. Cost 2 days over the existing 10 engines, 22 languages; output = one fused transcript + a per-item abstain flag. **Risk:** false-abstain rate; mitigate with plurality-without-quorum as LV-ROVER-MLT does. Novelty: **DERIVED**.

### F4 — Our leaderboard's mean CER is inflated by **abstention**, not repetition. Report median + abstention rate. **PRIMARY**
On all 12,324 `sheet.csv` rows: **paddleocr_indic 43.7% empty predictions, anuvaad_tesseract 50.3%, rapidocr 28.0%, surya 10.5%** (rest ≤0.2%). surya **mean 0.3944 vs median 0.3058, 13.2% of items at CER ≥ 0.999.** An empty prediction scores CER 1.0, so a quarter of surya's mean is refusal, not recognition.
Same pathology, different form. `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29213` (28 Jun 2026): corpus-mean error "is dominated by rare catastrophic repetition failures (outputs up to 71× the reference length) … which is why we report median and catastrophic-rate instead of the mean"; Table 3, blur — DeepSeek-OCR **mean 73.65 / median 1.50 / cat 2% / max× 71.6** vs Unlimited-OCR **6.42 / 2.40 / 1% / 3.8**. Repetition loops for VLM decoders, empty refusals for classical engines: one structural flaw in how this field reports OCR.
- Cost 0.5 day. Output: leaderboard columns `median_CER`, `abstain_rate`, `CER|matchescript`, `cat_rate`. Novelty: **PRIMARY** (our measurement); the reporting protocol is borrowed.

### F5 — Script-mismatch is a free, near-perfect error detector. Nobody has published it for multi-engine ensembles. **PRIMARY + DERIVED**
Measured on 12,324 rows (non-empty predictions only), CER when the engine's output shares a Unicode block with the GT vs when it does not: **surya 0.313 (match) vs 0.963 (mismatch), n=1098; tesseract_indic 0.483 vs 0.886, n=1224; tesseract_bilingual 0.482 vs 0.887; openbharatocr 0.484 vs 0.886; easyocr 0.490 vs 0.852; indicphotoocr 0.453 vs 0.904. paddleocr_indic (n=691) and anuvaad_tesseract (n=610) match 100% of the time — they abstain instead. doctr has no signal (0.878 vs 0.849, n=1226).** A 1-line Unicode-block check separates surya's good rows from its catastrophic rows with almost no overlap. GlotOCR Bench (`https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.12978v1`, 14 Apr 2026, LMU/TU Munich) makes this a headline metric — **ScriptAcc**, "a diagnostic for cross-script hallucination" — and finds hallucination is *not* random: "hallucination targets concentrate on a small set of high- and mid-resource attractor scripts, with Latin, Arabic, and Devanagari collectively accounting for the majority of cross-script substitutions." **GlotOCR applies it to VLMs. Nobody applies it to a 10-engine local ensemble — the configuration a solo operator actually has.** We could build (1 day): `script_agreement` as a per-item per-engine routing/abstain feature, no model, no download; feeds F3's gates and F4's abstain rate. Novelty: **DERIVED**.
