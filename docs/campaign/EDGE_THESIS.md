**Lead:** Sonnet session 2026-09-29 · **Protocol:** memory proto-15 (hunt) + proto-16 (refutation) · **Lens reports:** `checkpoints/W1_reports/1E_lensA.md`, `1E_lensB.md`, `1E_lensC.md` · **Hunt:** `W1_reports/1E_hunt.md`
**Law:** every number carries set, n and metric. No training, no downloads, no Sarvam calls. `sheet.csv` read-only.

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

**Four edge-thesis candidates were drafted and all four were killed.** Survival rule (proto-16 §2): a candidate survives only if **fewer than 2 of 3** adversarial lenses return REFUTED. C1 (script-block attribution) took 2 REFUTED + 1 WOUNDED; C2 (akshara-validity-gated fusion) took 3 REFUTED; C3 (script-mismatch abstention signal) took 2 REFUTED + 1 WOUNDED; C4 (GT script-purity gate) took 2 REFUTED + 1 WOUNDED. **Zero survivors.** That is the protocol's explicitly acceptable outcome. What the refutation did produce is more useful than a thesis: **one hard capability gap that all three lenses independently confirmed, and one evidence-integrity defect that the boss must know about before Wednesday.** Both are below.

**Killed-candidate reasoning, kept because the boss asked for the reasoning:**

**C1.** The claim was that our Santali cell is *saturated* — that a perfect Ol Chiki recogniser would score CER ≈ 0.457 against our best engine's 0.459, so the cell cannot move. The saturation story is false. The lead's own re-measurement (`sheet.csv`, `sat`, 10 local engines × 20 items = 200 rows, Ol Chiki share computed per row from Unicode block names): **mean Ol Chiki share 0.6764, median 0.6569 → the perfect-Ol-Chiki floor is ≈ 0.324, not 0.457**; best local engine on `sat` is `indicphotoocr` at **0.6514** (n=20), so the cell has ~0.33 of headroom. But the *reason* it cannot move is blunter than any metric novelty: **0 of 200 local-engine predictions contain a single Ol Chiki character**, and `mixed_script` is `False` on all 12,324 rows. No local engine emits the script at all. Per-script-block scoring therefore cannot discriminate — it returns 0.95 for every engine. And Sarvam, the competitor, already scores at the block level, so the measurement idea is not an edge either.

**C2.** This was the most promising thesis on paper — a real, measured fusion ceiling. It died on a measurement, not an argument: an akshara-validity argmax is **worse than always using surya** (0.6766 vs 0.4190). The reason is diagnosable: in OCR the oracle gap is dominated by cases where one engine is right and the others emit *nothing* or the *wrong script*, and an orthography-validity rule cannot pick the winner there — the right answer is not the valid-looking one. The published mechanism it borrowed from is also a poor transfer: LV-ROVER-MLT fused **5 streams of one recogniser** (Tesseract 5) on 57 Maltese pages with a Maltese lexicon, and its own result is one failed cross-dataset transfer.

**C3.** A good idea killed by its own statistics. The detector is nearly perfect on the rows it sees and silent on the rows that matter: recall 0.0–5.2% for 8 of 10 engines, and **exactly zero gain on Kashmiri, Urdu and Santali** — because Nastaliq is script-invariant, so the strongest script-mismatch signal is definitionally silent on the cursive scripts that are our worst cells. Routing surya on the signal *lowers* CER (−0.0351). The one piece worth keeping is cheap and is not an edge: **report median and abstain rate instead of mean** (lens C prices it at 2 hours; our mean CER is inflated by abstention — `paddleocr_indic` 43.7% empty, `anuvaad_tesseract` 50.3%, `rapidocr` 28.0%, `surya` 10.5%).

**C4.** The measurement is real and the remedy is wrong. The contamination exists, but the injected characters are too few to explain the CER gap, and removing them makes the score *worse*, not better. Worse, the 18 items are table pages (7.4× Latin, 7.9× digits) — the hardest and most representative Marathi items in the set. A gate that deletes them would report 0.105 instead of 0.205 **on a cell we currently win, and call it an honesty fix.** It is also blocked: `sheet.csv` is LOCKED and the boss deferred re-scoring (U5) to after the meeting. Do it as hygiene when U5 is answered, not before.

## 3. What survived the refutation: one capability gap, three lenses agree

All three lenses, from three different attack angles, landed on the same fact and it is reproducible:

- **No local engine can read Ol Chiki.** 0 of 200 local predictions on `sat` contain any Ol Chiki codepoint (lead re-measurement). Only Sarvam emits it (1A: Sarvam CER 0.026 on the one `sat` item where it transcribed both scripts).
- **No local engine can read Meetei Mayek either** — the same structural gap, n unmeasured but the traineddata inventory proves it: `level2/probe22/tessdata/` holds 12 files (`asm ben eng guj hin mar nep ori pan san snd urd`) and **none of `mni`, `sat`, `kan`, `mal`, `tam`, `tel`**.
- **The binding constraint is fonts, not models.** On this machine `NotoSansOlChiki-Regular.ttf` = 1 and `NotoSansMeeteiMayek-Regular.ttf` = 1, against Tamil 3, Telugu 4, Devanagari 5+. Our two dead languages have a single typeface each.
- **The likely fix is one file, and it is a download.** `https://github.com/indic-ocr/indic-ocr.github.io` — Tesseract models for Indic scripts including Ol Chiki and Meetei Mayek, ~2–4 MB, no training. **This is the single highest-leverage approval request in Wave 1, and it is a boss decision, not ours.** UNRESOLVED: the hunt did not open the pack's file listing to confirm a `sat`/`mni` traineddata file exists. Verify before the boss is asked to approve.

**This is not an accuracy edge and is not claimed as one.** It is a *coverage* edge: the only place on our board where we can state, as a measured fact, that an engine does not read a script at all — which is exactly the kind of honest, checkable claim the field currently cannot make, because every Indic OCR benchmark scores whole pages and `mixed_script` is `False` on all 12,324 of our own rows.

## 4. Evidence-integrity defect found during refutation (not a thesis — a blocker for the boss)

**The `CER` column in `level2/probe22/sheet.csv` cannot be re-derived from the `gt` and `prediction` columns in the same row using the repo's own scorer.** Lead measurement: seeded 400-row sample (`random.seed(20260926)`), `level2/probe22/metrics.py` imported and `calculate_cer` run on each row's own `gt`/`prediction`:

- matches the stored column within 1e-9: **66/400**; within 5e-4: **100/400**. 334/400 do not match, and 0 match `calculate_cer_uncapped` instead.
- No normaliser variant closes the gap: repo scorer 100/400 · NFC-both 55 · NFKC-both 55 · raw edit distance 54 · `calculate_wer` 65.
- The implied reference length `edit_distance / stored_CER` has median **1.336×** the stored GT length (p25 1.09, p75 2.00, max 6.24) — inconsistent, so this is not one systematic truncation.
- Mismatch is spread across all 11 models (doctr 33/37, easyocr 42/48, surya 34/40, tesseract_indic 32/37 …), i.e. it is not an artefact of the abstaining engines.
- The likely cause is that `sheet.csv`'s `gt`/`prediction` columns are not byte-identical to the pack text that was actually scored (the pack JSONs carry both `regions[text]` and `regions[text_nfc]`), or the column was written by a different scoring pass. NOT established.

**What this does and does not mean.** It does **not** mean the campaign's numbers are wrong: every headline figure — the 10/18 Sarvam paired result, the 9-of-12 winner count at n≥50, the kok gap 0.1938 — is computed **from** the stored column, so they are internally consistent and mutually reproducible (1A and 1G's verifier both re-derived them from the column and they held). It **does** mean the sheet we point a reviewer at cannot regenerate our own number with our own code, which is the first thing a sharp technical reviewer will try. Tag: **CONTRADICTION** — sheet.csv's CER column vs `metrics.py`. Decision it can change: whether `sheet.csv` is a citable evidence artefact at all, or only an internal index. **Not fixable tonight**: regenerating it is LOCKED and is boss decision U5 ("score the 56 additions and regenerate the LOCKED `sheet.csv`" — recommendation: yes, after the meeting).

## 5. What we would need to find one

1. **An approval, not an idea.** The Ol Chiki / Meetei Mayek traineddata pack (§3) is the only find in this wave that is cheap, specific, and blocked purely on a download decision.
2. **A comparison the incumbents refuse to make.** Sarvam scores itself on a benchmark it built, macro-averaged over 22 languages with English excluded and degenerate outputs dropped (1D, PRIMARY from the HF card). Nobody in the field publishes handwriting, tables or code-mixed conditions for Indic — 0% table, 0% mixed-script, 100% printed in *our* 1,283 items too. A 22-language table with abstention rate, median and per-condition breakdown would be genuinely new **and** it is buildable from data we already hold. It is a credibility edge, not an accuracy edge, and must be presented as such.
3. **A cell where the oracle gap is real and reachable.** The fusion ceiling is real (per-item oracle **0.3213** vs best single **0.4190** on a seeded 300-item sample — *the hunt's original 0.4277/0.5229 pair did not reproduce and is withdrawn; lens B re-measured it*) but lens B showed 67% of it is the single `sa` cell, where surya emits 100% empty predictions — a bug, not a fusion problem. Ex-`sa` the gap is 0.0332. **There is no fusion edge here until the abstention bug is fixed.** That is a 1-day bug fix with a measured payoff, and it is the most concrete engineering item this wave produced.
