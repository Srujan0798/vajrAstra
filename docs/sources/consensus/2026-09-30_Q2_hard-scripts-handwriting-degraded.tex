    \section*{Lowest-CER \textbf{OCR Methods} on \textbf{Hard Indic Documents}}
    
    
    The lowest reported character error rates on the hardest Indian OCR cases come from script-specific deep sequence models for Urdu, transformer or CRNN-style handwritten recognizers for broader Indic scripts, and layout-aware pipelines for tables and forms; the biggest measured gains from restoration come before OCR, where enhancement often cuts CER by double-digit relative margins and sometimes by more than half.
    
    
    \textbf{Figure 1:} Consensus on OCR method and enhancement effects
    The meter points strongly toward yes. Across hard scripts, degraded scans, and structured documents, both architecture choice and preprocessing change OCR accuracy materially, although the evidence is much stronger for Urdu, Devanagari, and degraded scans than for Kashmiri, Meitei Mayek, or Ol Chiki.
    
    
    \subsection*{Lowest CER by Case}
    
    
    The literature splits into six practical subproblems: Urdu Nastaliq, other low-resource scripts, handwritten Indic text, structured government documents, degraded or historical scans, and preprocessing before OCR.

    Urdu printed Nastaliq has the clearest lowest CER in this corpus: Efficient CRNN reports 0.91\% CER and 1.49\% WER on a 245,000-line multi-font Urdu corpus, improving on stronger recurrent and Vision Transformer baselines while using fewer parameters  \cite{Nasir2024Efficient}. Older printed Urdu systems were much less accurate: MDLSTM-based unconstrained Nastaliq text lines reached 94.97\% recognition accuracy, while CNN+MDLSTM on UPTI reached 98.12\% recognition accuracy, both strong for their time but not directly comparable to sub-1\% CER reporting  \cite{Naz2017Urdu}.
    
    
    For handwritten Urdu, ET-Network is the clearest CER leader in the supplied corpus, reporting a new state-of-the-art 5.27\% CER and 19.09\% WER across NUST-UHWR, UPTI2.0, and MMU-OCR-21  \cite{Hamza2024ET-Network:}. Paragraph-level BharatOCR instead reports character recognition rates of 96.24\%, 92.05\%, and 94.80\% on Urdu benchmarks, which is competitive but not directly convertible to CER  \cite{Dey2025Handwritten}.
    
    
    The corpus is thin for Kashmiri Nastaliq specifically. It contains synthetic and generated corpora for Kashmiri OCR and cross-domain support for Urdu-like scripts, but no Kashmiri Nastaliq paper here reports a direct end-to-end CER that can be ranked against Urdu systems  \cite{Nasir2021MMU-OCR-21:} \cite{Akoushideh2025Persian/Arabic} \cite{Rizvi2020Deep}.
    
    
    \subsection*{Handwritten Indic Scripts}
    
    
    Across handwritten Indic text, modern sequence models consistently beat older feature-engineered systems. A Hindi CRNN trained with synthetic half-characters, conjunct-rich words, and degradation augmentation reached 2.14\% CER and 7.96\% WER, clearly outperforming a ResNet-50 baseline at 3.27\% CER and 11.82\% WER  \cite{Kumar2026Enhancing}. A few-shot Hindi ResNet-Transformer model still needed more adaptation, landing at 6\% CER and 13.0\% WER  \cite{Equbal2025Few-Shot}.
    
    
    Indic multilingual handwritten OCR remains harder than single-script benchmarks. The ICDAR 2023 competition winner averaged 95.94\% character recognition rate and 88.31\% word recognition rate across ten languages, showing strong but not near-saturated performance at page scale  \cite{Mondal2023ICDAR}. PLATTER formalizes this as a two-stage page-level problem and highlights that evaluation inconsistency, detection, and data scarcity remain major bottlenecks across ten Indic languages  \cite{Kasuba2025PLATTER:}.
    
    
    PARSeq-style transfer models now appear strongest across many Indic handwriting benchmarks, with state-of-the-art performance across most of ten languages and measurable benefit from transfer learning from printed to handwritten text  \cite{Lalitha2025Enhancing}. Semi-supervised learning also helps when labels are scarce: SemiHastakshar shows that a small labeled set plus large unlabeled handwriting pools improves robustness across writers  \cite{Evani2025SemiHastakshar:}.

    Ancient and degraded handwritten manuscripts are still much harder. A hybrid deep model for ancient Devanagari and Maithili reached 96.97\% and 95.83\% character accuracy  \cite{Jindal2023A}, while a semi-self-supervised variant improved over prior state of the art by 2.27\% and 3.48\% using only 5\% labeled data plus 60\% unlabeled data  \cite{Jindal2024A}.
    
    
    \subsection*{Low-Resource Scripts Beyond Devanagari}
    
    
    Evidence is sparse for Santali Ol Chiki, Manipuri Meitei Mayek, and Odia, and almost none of it reports CER rather than accuracy. That is the biggest gap in this literature.
    
    
    For Ol Chiki, the supplied corpus only reports handwritten digit recognition, not full OCR. A stacked CNN plus SIFT reached 99.13\% validation accuracy on a 5,200-sample custom digit dataset  \cite{Mukherjee2025Deploying}. This shows the script is learnable at the isolated-digit level, but it does not answer full-line OCR.
    
    
    For Odia, recent handwritten compound-character recognizers reach 96.64\% standard-test accuracy with DenseNet-121 and 92.29\% cross-writer accuracy with EfficientNet-B0  \cite{Sahoo2026Handwritten}. Simpler CNN baselines reach 95.6\% on handwritten characters  \cite{Panda2022CNN}, while broader compound-character systems report 86.56\% to 88.76\% on 112- to 120-class tasks  \cite{Dey2022Offline} \cite{Panda2022Complex}. Printed Odia work identifies nested CNNs as best in error rate and accuracy among tested deep networks, but the excerpt does not provide a CER number  \cite{Pattanayak2020Performance}.
    
    
    The provided papers do not contain a direct Manipuri Meitei Mayek OCR benchmark. That absence itself is informative: several surveys note that research coverage across Indian scripts is highly uneven, with far more mature work for Devanagari, Bangla, Telugu, and Urdu than for lesser-resourced scripts  \cite{Kataria2018Review} \cite{Sharma2020Offline}.

    \subsection*{Tables, Forms, and Government Documents}
    
    
    For structured government documents, exact full-text OCR is often not the main metric; field extraction and table reconstruction matter more. In Indian production settings, Parichay achieves 89.8\% exact match on nine government document types using supervised instruction fine-tuning on about 21,000 annotated samples  \cite{Faraz2026Designing}. It is optimized for Aadhaar-like multi-page, layout-variable records rather than generic page transcription  \cite{Faraz2026Designing}.
    
    
    Chitrapathak-2 is stronger for multilingual printed Indic OCR, staying close to Gemini-2.5 Flash across nine Indic languages and achieving state-of-the-art performance for Telugu, but it still struggles on forms and index-like layouts where ordering errors and missed or merged lines remain common  \cite{Faraz2026Designing}. For multilingual printed documents with tables, a YOLO-based layout analysis plus CNN recognizer achieved a CER of 0.01 on Devanagari versus 0.63 for PP-OCRv3 in one comparison, and its table pipeline reduced segmentation errors and improved final recognition accuracy  \cite{Li2025Printed}.
    
    
    Adaptive routing helps on mixed government corpora. Across 15,000 government documents, adaptive OCR engine selection improved accuracy by 23.7\% over single-engine baselines  \cite{Zhang2026Adaptive}. Table-specialized systems also matter: TC-OCR reported 78\% OCR accuracy with about a 25\% gain over Table Transformer, while ClusTi reached F1 scores of 87.5\% to 98.5\% on scanned and PDF table datasets  \cite{Anand2023TC-OCR:} \cite{Zucker2021ClusTi:}.
    
    
    \subsection*{Old, Degraded, and Historical Scans}
    
    
    Historical and degraded scans show the biggest performance spread and the biggest room for improvement. For challenging classical Indic print, a Sanskrit-specific attention LSTM reached 3.71\% CER and 15.97\% WER on long-word, degraded documents  \cite{Dwivedi2020An}. A fine-tuned TrOCR pipeline for archived Indian government documents reported 7.01 CER and 14.21 WER on low-resolution, faded, degraded text  \cite{Meghana2025Enhanced}.
    
    
    Domain adaptation also helps when labels are scarce. Label-free adaptation of Indic printed OCR pushed Hindi to 92\% WRR, Kannada to 93\%, and Punjabi to 77\% on degraded target documents, far above EasyOCR and Tesseract baselines  \cite{Manna2025Label-Free}. The same paper shows same-script initialization matters enormously: Hindi-pretrained weights reached 92.35\% WRR versus 51.93\% for cross-lingual Punjabi initialization and 6.01\% from scratch  \cite{Manna2025Label-Free}.
    
    
    Real degraded scans remain a stress test even for modern OCR-VLMs. On Devanagari, synthetic renders compressed systems into a narrow range, but real scans made nine of ten systems collapse, with EasyOCR dropping from chrF++ 93.6 to 58.3 and the open Qwen3-VL-8B outperforming GPT-5.5 on this script-specific benchmark  \cite{Singh2026Can}.
    
    
    This trajectory has moved from classical segmentation-heavy OCR toward script-specific deep sequence models, then toward layout-aware and VLM-based systems.

    \subsection*{Effect of Restoration Before OCR}
    
    
    Image restoration before OCR usually improves recognition, often substantially. The largest direct CER result in this corpus is PreP-OCR, which reduced CER by 63.9\% to 70.3\% relative on 13,831 pages of real historical documents by combining restoration and post-OCR correction  \cite{Guan2025PreP-OCR:}. On document deblurring, NAF-DPM achieved 1.55\% CER, versus 2.78 for DocDiff, 5.44 for Hradis, and 6.87 for DE-GAN, nearly halving CER relative to the next-best method  \cite{Cicchetti2024NAF-DPM:}.
    
    
    On forms and administrative documents, restoration also helps materially. A template-guided restoration system for degraded tax forms reached CER 0.1091 and increased full-page word coverage on 37 real documents by 4.15 to 18.05 percentage points across three OCR engines  \cite{PeñaRojas2026A}. A retail-bill adaptive enhancement pipeline reduced CER to 18.4\%, a 26.4\% improvement over raw Tesseract, while also improving WER by 31.2\%  \cite{Gaikwad2026Benchmarking}.
    
    
    Simpler preprocessing can still matter. A receipt-enhancement pipeline lowered average CER by up to 11 points  \cite{Auad2024A}. Deskewing, layout analysis, and preprocessing raised Tesseract word accuracy by 23\% on complex scanned documents  \cite{Pham2022IMPROVED}. Convolution-guided preprocessing lifted Tesseract character-level accuracy from 0.134 to 0.616 on a hard dataset, a 359\% relative gain  \cite{Sporici2020Improving}.

    The same direction appears in lower-resource or Indic-adjacent settings. Enhancement pipelines produced 10 to 20 percentage-point OCR accuracy gains and 10 to 25\% CER reductions in a manuscript-preservation framework  \cite{Paulson2026Digital}. On low-resolution Garhwali textbook images, super-resolution helped low-resolution pages but hurt high-resolution ones, with best accuracies of 87.44\% for ImageMagick plus super-resolution on low-resolution images and 88.13\% for Otsu without super-resolution on high-resolution images  \cite{Rawat2021ANALYSIS}.
    
    
    Unsupervised enhancement also helps noisy documents. A BiLSTM-enhanced CycleGAN improved Tesseract word accuracy on noisy receipts by up to 23.32\% over the original noisy images and 61.66\% over standard CycleGAN  \cite{Singh2024Unpaired}. Angle correction, font-size normalization, and line-curvature elimination improved Tesseract accuracy by 2.8\% on challenging Persian-like documents, showing that geometric cleanup remains useful even with stronger recognizers  \cite{Fateh2023Enhancing}.
    
    
    \subsection*{Bottom Line}
    
    
    For the hardest Indian OCR cases in this corpus, the lowest explicit CERs are 0.91\% for printed Urdu Nastaliq, 5.27\% for handwritten Urdu, 2.14\% for handwritten Hindi words, and 3.71\% for degraded classical Sanskrit print. Odia, Ol Chiki, and especially Meitei Mayek remain under-benchmarked in CER terms, so the field still lacks fair cross-script comparisons for some of the scripts the question asked about.
    
    
    The single most important shift is that script-specific deep sequence models plus layout-aware pipelines now outperform generic OCR on hard Indic documents. The single biggest open question is not model scale, but whether comparable low-CER results can be achieved on truly low-resource scripts and real degraded pages, rather than on isolated characters or cleaner benchmark subsets.
